What changed here

  1. One cash figure, correctly labelled. The masthead carried ₹180 / cash held computed as collected − deposited; the settlement card further down read a different field. Now To collect today and Cash in hand sit together, both from the accountability record, with the reconciliation note underneath. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:549 · :565 · RiderRunsScreen.tsx:95
  2. The next action is pinned, not scrolled to. Complete stop 3 and Call live in a dock outside the scroll area. The old stickyPrimary and finishButton were plain views inside the ScrollView, so a 7-stop run pushed them below the fold. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:555, :557, :563 · styles :785
  3. The next stop is named, not numbered. NEXT STOP · 3 becomes the customer and the door, and the money owed is on the same line. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:558
  4. Counts reconcile. 2 of 7 delivered with an explicit legend for done / remaining / retry / return, instead of a bare 5 remaining · 1 retry that silently drops RETURN_REQUIRED. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:545, :547 · subscriptionOperationsService.ts:16-26
  5. The OTP contradiction is gone. The old sheet said No OTP needed for every cash stop, because Boolean(selectedStop.subscriptionDelivery) is always true — while the proof block in the same sheet said with customer OTP. Now one row owns the proof requirement, and it agrees with itself. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:576 (dead branch) vs :171

state: runQuery.isLoading

3 · Empty — nothing left on the route

state: all stops terminal

The shipped screen has no empty state at all: when every stop is delivered it still renders the Ordered stops heading and a Finish route button, with no explanation of what happens to the cash already collected. The proposal states the outcome and the next step — which matters most on the last run of the day, when the rider still owes the store.

4 · Error & offline

state: runQuery.isError / no network

:533 renders one message — “Route unavailable” — for every failure, and :532 a full-screen spinner for every load. The screen already imports RiderRunOfflineQueue and replays queued actions on reconnect (:247-252), but there is no offline state to show for it. The proposal splits the two: a network drop keeps the cached manifest and offers Sync now; a genuine failure explains what failed and what is still safe.

Why this split matters

  1. Offline is not an error. The app already has the data and the queue. Showing a dead-end for a tunnel costs a rider a whole stop. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:57, :253-260, :532-533
  2. The queued count is visible. A rider who can see “2 stops recorded offline” knows the work is safe; today only a toast says so, and it disappears. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:553
  3. Failure states say what is safe. The proposal names the run, says whether money is at risk, and gives two real routes forward, instead of one “Retry”.

5 · The stop sheet — completing a delivery

state: sheet open on stop 3

The sheet is where the run is actually finished, and today it opens with the wrong thing at the top: the header and address, then roughly 140 lines of extra-milk form (quantity chips, rate field, day chips, note, summary), and only then the primary action. Extra milk is the exception; completing the delivery is the rule. The proposal inverts that order and makes the exceptional path a disclosure that is one tap away — not deleted, just no longer in the way. It also gives every chip a real 44pt target and a radio-group role, and replaces placeholder-only inputs with visible labels. Reordering the sheet

  1. Complete first. The primary action now sits directly under the address and the money, at 54pt, before anything optional. Today it is last, after ~140 lines of form. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:580-725 vs :751
  2. Extra milk is a disclosure, not a wall. Same fields, same order, still reachable in one tap — but collapsed. Nothing is removed.
  3. Chips are radios. 44pt minimum, role="radio" inside a labelled radiogroup with aria-checked, so a screen reader announces “2 L, radio, 2 of 4” rather than “2 L, button”. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:619-626, :662-671, :762
  4. Inputs have labels, not placeholders. Every field carries a visible label; the placeholder is an example, in #5A6B62 at 5.65:1 rather than #94A3B8 at 2.56:1. RiderRunDetailScreen.tsx:557, :643, :653, :682, :747, :750, :763, :772
  5. Failure is separated and worded plainly. “Report a problem with this stop” sits below the note, visually distinct, so it is never mistaken for the happy path.
